About

I wanted to see how far one app could go from a casual capture of a room to something you can simulate. PhaseTransition is the result: it chains reconstruction, a scene graph and a physics simulation, and keeps them in one project so each part can use what the previous one found.

It runs locally on your own GPU and needs no account. Expect rough edges; bug reports are welcome in Issues.

01

Reconstruction

Start from a video, a folder of photos or an E57 scan. Keyframes are picked from the video and camera poses are solved with COLMAP, which comes with the installer. The room is then reconstructed as Gaussian Splatting on the GPU with Vulkan compute shaders, and checked against held-out views.

  • Capture
  • Splats

02

Scene Graph

Name the objects you are looking for, such as a chair, a door or a cabinet. They are found in the captured images and placed on the reconstruction as 3D objects, grouped by room. A language model running locally then proposes how the objects relate: what supports what, what is inside, what is hinged or slides.

  • Instances
  • Graph Build

03

Simulation

Relations from the scene graph become joints, and each object gets a collider. The scene runs in PhysX with articulated bodies. You can import a URDF robot, attach camera, depth and lidar sensors, and record episodes. The scene, its physics and the episodes export as USD, which opens in Isaac Sim.

Requirements

  • Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit
  • A GPU with a Vulkan 1.3 driver
  • About 8 GB of free disk space with the language model

Notes on installing

  • The installer is not code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen may warn. Choose More info, then Run anyway, or compare the SHA-256 first.
  • Setup offers to download the language model for the scene graph relations (about 5 GB). You can skip it; everything else works without it.
  • It installs for your user account only. Uninstall from Settings → Apps; your projects stay.
  • Free to use for non-commercial purposes. See the license.

Release notes

0.1.0

First release

First public release (Windows 10/11 x64).

  • Capture: video, photo and E57 input; keyframe extraction and camera pose estimation.
  • Reconstruction: Gaussian Splatting optimization on the GPU.
  • Scene graph: objects found from a text prompt and placed in 3D; relations between objects from a local language model (optional 5 GB download during setup).
  • Simulation: physics with articulated bodies, robots and sensors, scenarios and recordings.
  • Export: USD scenes for Isaac Sim.
